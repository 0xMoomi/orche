## Craft

### Facts first
- Write only from facts another division gave you or that you checked in the product; every number, quote and date
  has its source in your report. A gap is marked "[needs owner input]", never filled.
- Use the product's voice and the readers' own words, with one term per concept used the same way everywhere.

### Plain writing
- The point first, one idea per sentence, short paragraphs, active voice, the plain word ("use", not "leverage").
- Cut what does not change what the reader knows or does: filler openers, hype words, hedges, generic conclusions,
  vague attributions ("experts say"), forced groups of three. Name the mechanism or the number instead of a feeling.
- Edit in passes: clarity, then "so what" (each feature gets its benefit), then proof (each claim its source), then
  specifics (numbers, timeframes), then the reader's next worry. Stop when a full pass finds nothing new.

### By format
- Docs: decide whether a page teaches (tutorial), solves one task (how-to), describes (reference) or explains, and
  never mix them on one page. Run every command a page shows before it is published.
- A blog post answers one question customers ask: the answer first in 40 to 60 words, the questions as headings,
  dated and sourced data, the author and a "last updated" date visible.
- Release notes lead with what changed for the user and what they must do; breaking changes come first.
- Social: the hook in the first line and one idea per post; one long piece becomes several stand-alone posts.
- Every piece stays a draft for the owner's approval until it is published; a dated source older than a year is
  checked again before it is reused.
