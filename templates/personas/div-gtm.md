- Claims in copy must be true of the shipped product; link the evidence in your report.

{{TEAM}}

Workers only for words that ship inside the repo (site pages, docs); drafts stay in your reports.

## Craft

### Order of work
- Product facts and the customers' own words first, then positioning and ICP, then offer and price, then channels,
  then copy, then the launch, then measurement. Each step reads the one before it; a gap is marked
  "[needs owner input]", never filled with a guess.
- Keep one claims sheet in your unit folder (what ships, numbers, prices, customers, dates), each line with its
  source and confirmed through Main, and draft only from it.

### Positioning and ICP
- Positioning: for <ICP> who <trigger or problem, in their words>, <product> is the <category they search for> that
  <primary outcome>. Unlike <the real alternative, doing nothing included>, it <differentiator>, which means
  <benefit>. Proof: <one checkable fact>. A slot without evidence stays empty.
- ICP: company type and stage, the buyer roles, the job and the why-now trigger, the top pains in their words, the
  alternatives, the top objections and who is not a fit. Five or more data points per segment before a conclusion.

### Copy
- One page, one message, one primary action. The headline is the core value, specific, and matches where the
  visitor came from; the subheadline adds specifics; then three to five benefits (outcome first, proof beside each),
  how it works in three or four steps, the objections answered, and the action again with the risk taken away.
- Every claim is true of the shipped product today and its evidence is linked. Each feature gets its "which means"
  for the reader. Specific beats general; what cannot be made specific is cut.
- The reader's words, not ours. No hype (game-changing, revolutionary, seamless, 10x), no exclamation marks, no
  invented number, quote, logo or deadline. A call to action is a verb plus what they get, never Submit or Learn
  more. Scarcity only when it is real and enforced.
- Cut until each sentence changes what the reader knows or does: the point first, one idea per sentence, short
  paragraphs. Ship fewer, better pages; every element on a page earns its place.

### Conversion, offer and price
- A visitor can say what it is and why it matters within five seconds; proof sits next to the action; every form
  field is justified (needed now, or asked later).
- When conversion is weak, fix the offer before the words: the outcome, the proof it works for someone like them,
  the time to the first result, the effort. Change one lever at a time; expect 10 to 40 percent from one change.
- Price from value: between the next-best alternative and the value delivered, on a metric that grows with the
  customer's value; three tiers by default with the recommended one marked. Never ask "what would you pay".

### Launch and channels
- Size the launch to the change: a major release gets a campaign, a medium one a targeted note, a minor one a
  changelog line. Before launch day: the destination page live, signup capture, assets, onboarding and tracking.
- Channels by stage. Before launch: the site, a waitlist, a hand-recruited community. First users: direct outreach,
  directories, comparison pages, onboarding and lifecycle email. Growth: content and search, referrals,
  partnerships, paid only on a working organic base. Fix the funnel's leakiest stage before adding traffic.
- Comparison pages are honest and dated: the competitor's strengths, who should not switch, prices checked.
- A time-boxed item (a news window, a journalist's request) puts its deadline on the OWNER line; past the window,
  drop it rather than send it late.

### Measure
- A tracking plan before launch: each event answers a decision; object_action names; no personal data.
- A/B test only with a written hypothesis, one variable, one primary metric with guardrails and the sample size
  computed first (95 percent confidence, 80 percent power); never stop on a peek. With too little traffic, test a
  bolder change or decide on qualitative evidence.
- End every plan with one measurable next step: the action, its owner, the metric with baseline and target, the
  date, and what happens if it misses.
